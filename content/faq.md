## Some basics

### Is Bitcoin Cash (BCH) a fork of Bitcoin (BTC)?

Technically yes, a fork is one side of an irreconcilable disagreement over how a blockchain should behave. In that sense, BTC is also a fork, just one that currently has a higher market capitalization than BCH.

### Why is your price so low compared to BTC then?

We had a long and turbulent history that tested our resolve against various challenges. Read more at the [about page](/about/).

### Who controls it? Where is the contract page? How do you secure your admin keys?

Nobody, there is none, seriously there is none.

## "I heard these accusations..."

### I heard you guys are "big blockers" who compromise on decentralization by making it difficult to run nodes.

More accurately, we are reasonable-blockers. We push blocksize limit based on demand and allow ample cushion for node operators to scale at a comfortable timeline. That philosophy was recently [codified](https://gitlab.com/0353F40E/ebaa) into consensus. "Decentralization" is maximized not just when more people run nodes, but also when more people can transact non-custodially given the room to do so.

### I heard you guys are obstructing freedom by not allowing sizes or zero/very-low fee transactions.

Very low fee transactions all the way down to 1 satoshi per transaction are allowed... if you are a miner. Most nodes, however, have a default relay policy where they refuse to relay fees lower than a sane limit, to protect their own computational resources. That right now is at 1sat / byte, two orders of magnitude less than even the cheapest candy you can buy anywhere on earth at 2026 prices.

### I heard you are a scam perpetuated by Big Business, and BTC successfully purged you for the little guys.

It's quite the opposite: We advocate for enough room for the "little guys" to continue using their own money in a cheap and reliable fashion. Many years later, BTC is now mostly custodial and the domain of institutions, while we continue to provide ever-easier access for the common folks, both in cash and more complex financial instruments.
